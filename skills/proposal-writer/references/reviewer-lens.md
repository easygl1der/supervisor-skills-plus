# Reviewer lens

Reviewers score against written criteria, often after a fast first read.
Quote the call's own criteria on the call sheet and score against those;
use the generic lens in section 2 only when the call gives none.

## 2. Generic lens

Six criteria, each scored 1 (exceptional) to 9 (poor) in the NIH
direction, or high, medium, low:

| Criterion | The reviewer asks | Where the answer must sit |
|---|---|---|
| Significance | If this succeeds, what changes, and for whom? | First page; rationale section |
| Innovation | What is new relative to the nearest work, on which axis? | Gap paragraph; each aim |
| Approach and rigor | Will the design answer the question? Are threats named? | Method section, per aim |
| Feasibility | Can this person do it with these resources in this time? | Preliminary data; foundation; timeline |
| Measurability | How will anyone know whether it worked? | Milestones; expected results |
| Risk handling | What fails first, and is there a way forward? | Risk register; per-aim fallback |

## 3. Heilmeier questions as a pre-check

Before scoring, answer the eight questions from the draft alone, quoting
the sentence that answers each. A question the draft cannot answer is a
finding.
[src: guide:https://www.darpa.mil/about/heilmeier-catechism@2026-10-16] (confirmed)

1. What are you trying to do, with no jargon?
2. How is it done today, and what are the limits of current practice?
3. What is new in the approach, and why should it succeed?
4. Who cares, and what difference will success make?
5. What are the risks?
6. What will it cost?
7. How long will it take?
8. What are the mid-term and final exams that check success?

For a thesis proposal, question 6 becomes "what resources does it need
and are they secured".

## 4. Reviewer pass procedure

1. **Skim test.** Read only the first page (or the summary). Write down the
   question, the gap, the approach and why this applicant. Any blank is a
   major weakness regardless of what later pages say.
2. **Score.** For each criterion on the call sheet, give a score and quote
   the sentence it rests on. No quote means the criterion is unanswered.
3. **Hostile reading.** For each aim, write the strongest one-sentence
   objection a specialist reviewer would raise, then check whether the
   draft already answers it.
4. **Top three weaknesses.** Rank by how likely each is to sink the
   proposal; give a concrete fix for each (move, add evidence, cut scope,
   add fallback).
5. **Consistency.** Aims, timeline, contributions and budget or resources
   name the same work; numbers agree across sections.

Write the pass to `review.md`; it never enters the proposal.

## 5. Frequent weaknesses

| Weakness | Symptom in the draft | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Fishing expedition | Aims say "explore", "investigate", "study" with no question | Rewrite each aim as a question or hypothesis with its evidence |
| Cascade failure | Aim 2 uses Aim 1's output with no alternative | Fallback input for Aim 2, or merge the aims |
| Asserted feasibility | "We are confident", "we have experience" | Pilot numbers, access letters, prior outputs |
| Unscoped gap | "No previous work", "first to" | Name the searches; scope to what was retrieved |
| Single-source gap | The whole gap rests on the limits of one paper | Retrieve the nearest follow-up and competing work before claiming the gap |
| Literature as a list | Paragraphs that summarize papers one by one | Organize by the limitation that motivates the project |
| Activity timeline | Months filled with "literature review", "experiments" | Deliverables with criteria that can fail |
| Overreach | More aims than the duration supports | Cut to the aims with feasibility evidence |
| Missing risk section | No E6 anywhere | Short register with fallbacks; reviewers read it as judgment |
