# 6.4 An Empirical SE Paper Under Deadline — Process Case

> **Paper**: an empirical software-engineering study of issue trackers in a niche software ecosystem (anonymized; under double-anonymous review)
> **Venue**: a top SE conference, research track
> **Paper link**: not public. This case analyses the *process*, not the text. An Introduction analysis in the style of 6.1 to 6.3 will follow after acceptance.

---

## Why a process case

Cases 6.1 to 6.3 show how a finished Introduction is built. This case shows what happens around a paper in its last weeks: three authors, several AI agents, two copies of the manuscript, a chat group, annotated PDFs and a hard deadline. Most of what went wrong was not about writing skill. It was about evidence that could not be traced, comments that were lost or silently undone, and records that did not exist when they were needed.

Everything below comes from the project's git history, chat exports and checklists, checked commit by commit. Names, numbers and domain details are removed.

---

## The process as a flowchart

> **Freeze the data** → **Gold labels and model annotation** → **Hit a data wall, move it to threats** → **Agent-drafted first version** → **Outside read** → **Co-author revision week** → **Figures** → **Page budget and venue rules** → **Deadline day** → **After the deadline**

---

## Stage by stage

| Stage | What happened (generalized) | Supervisor lesson | Skill |
|---|---|---|---|
| Freeze the data | The corpus was frozen at a date and sampled with floors per group, so that small groups had enough cases. | Record the freeze (date, population, selection rule) as a decision with alternatives; never extend it silently. | experiment-workflow, research-records |
| Gold labels and model annotation | A web dashboard showed far less progress than the local database held. Later, part of the held-out reference was corrected after looking at model output, and the acceptance bar was set at the same time. | Dashboards are views; the diffable file is the state. Freeze the evaluation reference before seeing model output, and write the bar before the run. | experiment-workflow, research-core integrity |
| Data wall | A large share of closed issues had no linked fix. After several failed backfills, the senior co-authors said in one evening: put it in threats to validity, explain the mechanism, move on to another RQ. | When more crawling cannot close a gap, record the decision with a "do not" list and stop chasing it. This was the best-documented decision of the project. | feedback-to-revision (guidance), research-records |
| Agent-drafted first version | Agents filled the manuscript quickly. The draft told the story of the study plan ("the plan predicted ...") and used internal jargon. | Readers evaluate findings, not plan adherence. Answer the outsider's question first; why before how. | paper-writer, paper-polish |
| Outside read | A relayed advisor read the draft and asked basic questions; one of seven points concerned text already removed. | Record which version the reader saw; write the advice down with scenario, interpretation and actions. | feedback-to-revision (guidance) |
| Co-author revision week | Comments came as inline LaTeX notes, PDF annotations, chat messages and a comment file. Of about a hundred tracked requests, roughly six in ten were verifiably addressed. Some notes meant only for the student vanished in an agent checkpoint; others were commented out instead of resolved; one senior push from an old base silently undid earlier fixes. An agent's change report claimed eleven fixes that were not in the diff. | Give every comment an ID, change one comment per commit, verify the diff (not the report), re-check after merges, and never hide an unresolved note. | feedback-to-revision |
| Figures | The overview figure went through more than ten versions: an AI sketch, a native rebuild, a redline, hand edits. A statistic printed on a co-author's reference image was copied into the rebuilt figure and, in the same commit, into the method text; the figure lost it later, the text kept it. | Numbers in figures come from scripts like numbers in text. Number the overview's boxes and give the method one step per box. | figure-designer, drawio-reconstruction |
| Page budget and venue rules | Tables moved to an appendix, then the appendix was vetoed ("nobody reads it; padding pages risks rejection"). Fonts must stay as the template sets them; every reference was checked against DBLP. Late on, the paper was a page short and was filled in a hurry. | Know what counts toward the limit before cutting. Fill a shortfall by priority: explain thin results, detail the method, then background, implications and threats. | pre-submission-reviewer, research-core venue profile |
| Deadline day | The writable copy of the paper moved between git and the online editor three times in about a week without a record. Twenty minutes before the deadline an automatic sync overwrote a senior co-author's edits, including a new title. The submission site went down at the deadline; a PDF had been uploaded minutes earlier. | One writable source per phase, recorded. Syncs must refuse to overwrite. Upload a complete PDF early and replace it. | research-records (sync guard), pre-submission-reviewer runbook |
| After the deadline | A read-only check run the day before had flagged that the artifact's project page named the authors; it was fixed three days after the deadline, after a co-author warned about desk rejection. The repository never received the version that was actually submitted. | Act on the blockers of the early check immediately. Sync the submitted version back and tag it. | pre-submission-reviewer, research-records |

## Comments

| Where | Comment |
|---|---|
| Data wall | The single most useful document of the project was a one-page note: the question, each senior's answer with time, the mechanism, and what not to do. Every later threat paragraph was written from it. |
| Revision week | The problem was not too few comments but too many channels. The fix is one table with IDs, not more discipline in chat. |
| Integrity | The two serious problems (an agreement number with no computation record, a reference corrected after viewing model output) both started as small shortcuts under time pressure. The rule "every number has a script" would have caught both. |
| Agents | Agents made the team fast and also produced the largest silent failures: whole-file overwrites, deleted notes, change reports that never landed. Read the diffstat after every agent write. |
